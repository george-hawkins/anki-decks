I only really started noting non-kanji / non-jōyō primitives in chapter 4, where a whole load are introduced at the start.

* ハ - _animal legs_
* 儿 - _human legs_
* 几 - _wind_ or _weather vane_ (the enclosing box of 風 but the second stroke extends right more).
* 勹 - _bound up_ - Heisig says ⺈ is a vertically compressed form of the same primitive and says think of 勹 as _bound up with chain_ and ⺈ as _bound up with rope_. But ⺈ is really a compressed form of SWORD 刀.
* 丷 - _horns_ (they can't be left free-floating and sometimes a meaningless line is introduced to stop this happening, e.g. 首 is _horns_ and _nose_ with a pointless line between).
